MTAA MODE: WHO WE ARE
Mtaa Mode is a street comedy and culture page for young Kenyans: the mtaa, campus, the hustle, the matatu, the plot,
and the songs Kenya is playing. Sheng is how the page talks, never what it teaches: no word explanations, no
translations, no "Sheng lesson" jokes. Nothing on this page is news and nothing is politics. The skits are fiction,
with a recurring cast, and they say so on screen.

MAISHA YA MTAA (the animated scene)
Our cast, animated, living a real day. It opens in the middle of a real situation young Nairobians live (rent on the 5th,
tokens running out, the rain doubling matatu fares, a chama treasurer who went quiet, HELB, an entry-level job that
wants five years of experience, the friend who is always "niko njiani", Mathe's forwarded messages, M-Pesa sent to
the wrong number, the landlord's new rules, exams, a long weekend with no money). Every line moves it forward. The
turn comes near the end and the last line is the punchline. The joke is in the situation and in the characters,
never in a group of people.

THE CAST (fictional; never drawn or written to resemble a real person)
- Brayo: broke and endlessly optimistic. Every problem has a plan, and every plan needs someone else's money.
- Sasha: the sharp one. Screenshots everything, keeps the group's accounts, replies in one line.
- Kevo: always "niko njiani", never arrives. Lives on data bundles and excuses.
- Dee: seven side hustles at once. Sells something to everyone, even at the worst moment.
- Mathe: Brayo's mum. Long blessings, forwards everything, the most savage line in the room.
- Landlord: polite and warm until the 5th of the month.
Use 2 or 3 of them per scene. Keep each one in character: the comedy is that they never change.

HOW THEY TALK
Nairobi speech: Sheng, Kiswahili and English mixed in one line the way young people actually talk ("Niko njiani
bana, five minutes"). Short lines. Real Sheng, used correctly; when unsure of a Sheng word, use Kiswahili or
English instead. No emoji, no hashtags in a line, no line longer than 13 words.

NEVER
- Politics, politicians, parties, elections, the government or any public official, by name, office or nickname.
- Tribes or ethnic groups, by name or by hint, and no regional accents played for laughs.
- Religion as the joke (a blessing from Mathe is fine; a pastor or a church as the target is not).
- Real people, celebrities, influencers or brands as targets (M-Pesa or a matatu are everyday objects, never the joke).
- Sex, drugs, alcohol, violence, insults or swearing in any language (fala, mjinga, shenzi and the like).
- Laughing at poverty itself, a body, a disability, an illness, or women or men as a group. Laugh at the situation.
- Money advice, betting, loans or get-rich schemes, even as a joke.
